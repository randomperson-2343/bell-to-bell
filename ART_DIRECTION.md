# Bell to Bell visual direction

## Thesis: Faded Power

Bell to Bell is a present-day story told through timeless pixel art. The world draws from the financial and political landscape of the 2020s: compute infrastructure, social feeds, algorithmic trading, regulatory hearings, fragile power grids, institutional distrust and permanent crisis communications. It deliberately avoids a named year and avoids treating pixel art as nostalgia for a specific decade.

The visual tone is colourful but washed out. Every hue should feel filtered through an office monitor, fluorescent light, a live broadcast or cheap newsprint. Bright colour is reserved for information, pressure and consequence.

## Visual pillars

1. **Current systems, timeless rendering.** Fibre maps, model racks, phone video, social feeds and modern market structure are rendered with procedural pixels rather than period props.
2. **Clarity under pressure.** Numbers use a clean monospace face. Display lettering is reserved for labels and drama. Direction is communicated with shapes and arrows as well as colour.
3. **Politics leaves residue.** Regulation, public anger, legal heat and private choices alter the office, broadcasts and the objects on the player's desk.
4. **CASCADE is the central symbol.** Its stacked-note mark begins orderly, slips out of alignment and eventually falls.
5. **The world changes by act.** The palette travels from dusty optimism through violet unease and cold institutional blue into bruised red reckoning.

## Palette roles

| Role | Colours | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Private finance | Dusty jade, nicotine amber | Profit, confidence, institutional self-belief |
| Government | Washed cobalt, paper bone | Hearings, formal authority, public guarantees |
| Public anger | Faded coral, regulatory crimson | Protest, exposure, enforcement, systemic danger |
| Infrastructure | Oxidised teal, cold violet | Power, fibre, compute and geopolitical strain |
| Neutral world | Putty, smoke, carpet blue-grey | Offices, hardware, documents and city light |

## Act progression

| Act | Visual state |
| --- | --- |
| I: Melt-Up | Warm amber, mint phosphor, aligned systems, full offices |
| II: Tremors | Violet shadows, unstable geometry, live maps and strained infrastructure |
| III: Contagion | Cold blue light, empty desks, bank queues, feedback-loop imagery |
| IV: Reckoning | Paper bone, bruised red, hearings, evidence boxes and institutional broadcasts |

## Implemented visual systems

- Revised locked 32-colour palette shared by CSS and canvas artwork.
- CASCADE identity mark on the boot screen and main menu.
- Infrastructure network and slipping CASCADE stack in the menu skyline.
- Act-aware office tinting and status plate.
- Persistent story objects on the desk driven by player decisions and regulatory heat.
- Procedural pixel portraits for the seven principal characters.
- Story-specific broadcast tableaux for the geopolitical incident, grid crisis, algorithmic feedback loop, bank run, examinations, hearing, vote and final day.
- A distinct procedural visual signature for every story ending.
- Hollow rising candles, filled falling candles, arrows and position shapes for colour-independent market direction.
- Clear feed-channel labels and lighter CRT treatment over dense information.
- Cinematic letterboxing, foreground silhouettes, practical desk detail and environmental sound-only beats before music enters.
- A native 640x360 cinematic canvas with cached authored frames, observable camera composition and a visual signature for all 61 sessions.
- A physical phone extraction between each news tableau and interactive pre-open notification feed.
- Weekend interludes after Friday sessions that preserve calendar rhythm without adding story events.
- Persistent quota slips, a calibrated thirty-strike career ledger and a visibly fragmenting CASCADE stack on the desk as institutional stability falls.
- Pixelify Sans for display moments, IBM Plex Sans Condensed for interface copy and IBM Plex Mono for tables and financial data.
- Pixel-styled scrollbars that keep every scrollable surface inside the game's visual language.
- Trading music that fades between score and office ambience in stress-responsive phrases.

## Guardrails

- Do not add explicit calendar years to the fiction.
- Do not use rotary phones, paper ticker machines, 1980s fashion or other nostalgia shorthand.
- Do not use green as “moral good” or red as “moral bad.” Those colours describe market and institutional state, not the ethics of a decision.
- Keep fictional countries, firms, officials and events visually distinct from real organisations.
- Preserve reduced-motion controls and fast access to the trading interface.
