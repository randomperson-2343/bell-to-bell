How to Play

The goal

Each trading day runs from the opening bell (9:30) to the closing bell (4:00), which takes about three real minutes. Make as much money as you can. Your boss sets a daily quota, and a weekly quota that resets every Monday: the whole week has to clear the sum of its daily quotas plus 15%. Every missed day and every missed week becomes a career strike, but making the weekly quota wipes one missed day from that week. Thirty strikes and you're done.

Your desk

  • The left monitor is the trading terminal: watchlist, chart, order ticket, and your positions.
  • The right monitor is comms: the Wire (real news), Sqwak (rumors, many of them fake, and big accounts can move a stock for a few minutes), your Inbox, and the phone when it rings.
  • The desk below holds the clock, your coffee, the stress gauge and the pause button.

Trading

  • Buy goes long and makes money when a stock rises. Sell closes a long, or goes short when you're flat and makes money when it falls.
  • Leverage: you can hold several times your equity during the day, but only half that overnight. Anything over is force-sold at the close.
  • Margin call: if equity falls too far, an alarm sounds and a countdown starts. Cut positions or risk will dump your worst ones for you.
  • Limit and stop orders wait for a price. SL% and TP% attach an automatic stop-loss and take-profit to a market order.
  • Options: calls bet it goes up, puts bet it goes down. You pay the premium in full, the payoff can be huge, and they expire.
  • FEAR tracks market panic. When it's high, spreads widen, options get expensive and slippage bites.

Phone calls

Clients pay commission for orders you work in time. Your boss calls to shout. And people call with tips — most of which do not work. Some are simply false. Some are true but already in the price. Some run your way just long enough to get you to size up, and then reverse. Acting on every tip is how you go broke.

Stress

Losses, leverage, margin calls, ringing phones and a looming close all push up your stress through Loaded, Tunnel and Critical stages. High stress can still cause a bounded fat-finger order. At 100, a short panic attack begins. Complete its A/S/D grounding sequence to return immediately. Recovery creates temporary resistance so attacks cannot chain. Going flat or taking a coffee break also brings stress down.

Saving

Press Esc any time to pause, and Save or Save & Quit — even in the middle of a session. You come back to the same minute, with the same positions and the same tape. You can keep six saves going at once.
